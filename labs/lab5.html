Lab

Firstly, we need to decide what controller specifically we want to implement. Each of P/I/D introduce more dimensions of control. In general, the P term reacts to present error, the I term reacts to accumulated error, and the D term reacts to the rate of change of error. In the context of a car avoiding an obstacle - the P term loosely dictates how quickly we can react, the I term dictates how precisely we can react, and the D term dictates how accurately we can react.

More specifically - P control alone will let us react, though the car will struggle to reach a target due to steady-state error. A well-tuned PI controller won't see any oscillatory behavior, but will probably still see an initial overshoot. A well-tuned PD controller might be slightly off target due to steady-state error, but will get to a target smoothly. A well-tuned PID controller will get to the intended target seeming seamlessly.

One thing that engineering school taught me is that the easiest solution tends to be the best solution for all parties involved, as long as it is 'good enough'. For this lab, I will define 'good enough' to mean stopping ~1 foot away from a wall after moving towards it at full speed, even if it takes a little bit to settle. With that, I will pursue P-only control for this lab, though I might investigate the I and D terms in the future if I feel that they are necessary.
