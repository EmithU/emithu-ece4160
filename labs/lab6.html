Lab

For Lab 5, slight oscillations are forgivable because the amplitude of oscillation is small relative to the size of the car itself. For this lab, they need to be mitigated at all costs because the effects of even small oscillations are significantly more noticable. I also think that labs 9-12 would benfit significantly from very precise orientiation control. As such, I theorized that P-only control will not be sufficient.

Loosely - P focuses on where the car is, I focuses on where the car has been in the past, and D focuses on where the car will be in the future. I orignally chose to pursue PD control specifically for this lab, as the car seemed to have very minimal steady-state error in lab 5 and this lab benefits heavily from a stronger garauntee of precision. In the end, I found that P-only control was sufficient. A broader explanation is presented at the end of the report.

As for the IMU - I like the idea of having an off-board chip do the sensor fusion work for me. It saves CPU cycles and minimizes yaw drift through algorithms I don't need to develop (assuming that I set up the system correctly). As such, I use the onboard DMP, following the directions linked on the lab website.

By doing this, I avoid many of the common pitfalls that come with raw accel/gyro data. Digital integration might miss events between two timesteps, and might also be suseptible to noise. This could be fixed by low-passing the signal. Furthermore, my sensor did not seem to have bias; if it did, a low-pass would once again help to 'dampen' the effects of the bias. The max rotational velocity that the gyroscope can read is +/- 2000 dps when confiugred at it's fastest mode, which is far faster than we would ever need.

Once again, the DMP avoids us making manual computations. That being said - it would not make sense for us to take the derivative of an integral, those are inverse operations. If we did have a D-term, changing a set point mid-run would be bad if it directly relied on the error term, as this instantaneous change would then be very large. This could be alleviated by only using sensor measurements for the D term. Even then, the D term would be suseptible to noise, and low passing it is certainly a good idea.

Issues Faced

I was facing issues with the car not being able to spin whatsoever. Turns out that this was due to friction between the ground and the car, and was fixed by placing tape over the wheels. Perhaps this would have helped in lab 5 as well, or maybe not due to the slippage. In any case, the tape fixed many of my problems with this lab instantly.
