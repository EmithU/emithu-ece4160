Lab 9

This lab seeks to map out a static room using the ToF sensors on the car.

Control

For this lab, I want to go beyond open-loop control. With that said, the idea of the car collecting ToF measurements while rotating sounds very hard to tune. As such, I plan on implementing orientation control. Additionally - I decided to aim for 15 readings, just because 360/15 is a clean 24.

While I implemented full PID control in lab 8, I did not spend too much time tuning it. Lab 8 required we make a precise enough turn quickly, but this lab demands precision above all else. Furthermore, the 'nature' of the turn in this lab is different; instead of a more 'continuous' turn, we want it to snap to a precise angle.

I have described the tuning process for these parameters in previous reports; essentially the same process was performed here. I found that a kp = 9.5, ki = 1.0, kd = 40, and integral wind-up clamp = 80 worked well in this case. Overall, the car does shift off-axis as it rotates, but is within acceptable limits. We can roughly say that the average error is ~3 inches (~76.2 mm), with a worst-case closer to 6 inches (152.4 mm), but this will vary between runs. While this error is considerable, the distances we are measuring are to the order of hundreds or thousands of mm; this rotation pattern should let us roughly map out the environment.

As for sensor drift, my use of the DMP mitigates the effects. When compared to the effects of the car's physical shifting, or erroneous noise, we can call the error from sensor drift negligible.
