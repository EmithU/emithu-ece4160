This lab seeks to perform a stunt with the car.

Controlled Stunts

We have the option of performing either a flip or a drift with the car. I chose to do a drift because it doesn't require a sticky mat to pull off.

This car needs to move fast. Furthermore, the car is prone to slipping; we need to be able to detect when to flip early to account for additional distance travelled slipping, and we need to be confident in our reading. This motivates our use of the Kalman Filter from lab 7 here, as it has proven to be capable of providing fast, capable readings.

The drift could really be broken down into two distinct phases. Phase 1 involves the initial journey towards the wall, while phase 2 involves the actual turn. Each phase has its own distinct set of challenges and utilizes different parts of the system developed throughout the course.

Phase 1

At first, the car needs to move fast. This is both to make the stunt a true 'stunt', and to give it enough momentum that it can later do a full drift. I will have the car start at a PWM of 150, and have it maintain this PWM once it gets close to the wall.

Next, we need to identify when the car is close to a wall. As mentioned previously, I use my Kalman filter developed in lab 7. I decided to initiate the drift when the car gets a distance reading of 700; this gives us ample time for the car to perform it's 180 degree turn.

Phase 2

At this point, the car has now detected that it needs to perform the turn. We first set the PWM of both motors to 0; the car should have enough linear momentum at this point to keep travelling forwards as it turns.

After this, we use our angular P-only PID algorithm to perform the 180 degree turn. Our use of the DMP, which can run at ~60Hz, should be fast enough to make the car turn roughly 180 degrees. Furthermore, the overshoot we saw in lab 6 will be essentially cancelled out by the linear momentum that the car has at this point. In theory, this should enable us to perform the turn.
