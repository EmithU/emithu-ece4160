Lab 3

This lab seeks to set up the Time of Flight (TOF) sensors.

Prelab

The ToF sensor used in this class, the VLF351X, has a hard-coded I2C address, which is 0x52 according to the datasheet.

By using two ToF sensors, we can get an understanding of the obstacles around the car across 2 axes; for the most part, the car will only move along two axes, so using two ToFs gives the car a relatively comprehensive understanding of what is going on around it.

Both of our ToF sensors are the same model, with a hard-coded I2C address out of the box. In order to use both, we need to change one of the I2C addresses. This can be done by using the XSHUT pin on one ToFs to toggle it off on the fly. This will let us change the address of the other. This process will need to be performed upon startup of the microcontroller everytime, but the alternative is toggling each ToF on/off continuously; this approach is considerably less error-prone and saves the constant CPU computation required.

As mentioned previously, we have 2 ToF sensors. I intend on placing one on the front of the car, and another on the right side of the car, between two wheels. This means that the car won't be able to see objects only along it's corners, but this specific situation should be relatively rare. We will also miss objects to the left and behind of us; we set the environment that the car will perform in, so we can just ensure that collisions due to the car not having vision of this area won't occur.

With this, it was decided that the short-distance mode will be used.
